Question: How do autonomy, asynchronicity, and agency together distinguish software agents from traditional automation?
Source score: 0.67
Abstention correct: True
Answer: Autonomy, asynchronicity, and agency together distinguish software agents from standard services or automation scripts [Source 2, Source 4]. These three interdependent principles enable software agents to operate with purpose, resilience, and intelligence in distributed, real-time environments [Source 4].

While autonomy (acting independently, reacting to inputs, and making decisions based on internal rules or objectives) and asynchronicity (participating in real-time systems and coordinating fluidly and efficiently) are necessary, they alone are not sufficient to make a system a true software agent [Source 1, Source 4]. The critical differentiator is agency, which introduces:
*   **Goal-directed behavior:** Agents pursue objectives and evaluate progress toward them.
*   **Decision-making:** Agents assess options and choose actions based on rules, models, or learned policies.
*   **Delegated intent:** Agents act on behalf of a person, system, or organization with an embedded sense of purpose.
*   **Contextual reasoning:** Agents incorporate memory or models of their environment to guide behavior intelligently [Source 1].

A system that is autonomous and asynchronous might still be a reactive service; what makes it a software agent is its ability to act with intention and purpose, to be agentic [Source 1].

Question: How did the actor model influence modern software agents?
Source score: 1.00
Abstention correct: True
Answer: The actor model significantly influenced modern software agents by providing their theoretical foundation, architectural paradigm, and conceptual grounding. Introduced by Carl Hewitt in the 1970s, the actor model treats computation as independent, concurrently executing entities (actors) that encapsulate their own state, interact through asynchronous message passing, and can create new actors and delegate tasks (Source 2, Source 4).

This model provided the conceptual foundation for decentralized reasoning, reactivity, and isolation, which are fundamental to the behavioral architecture of modern software agents (Source 4). Consequently, software agents are built on the principles of the actor model, enabling systems that are not only reactive but also proactive and purposeful, bringing autonomy, context-awareness, and intelligent delegation to modern computing (Source 3).

Question: What is the primary difference between traditional AI, software agents, and agentic AI?
Source score: 1.00
Abstention correct: True
Answer: The primary differences between traditional AI, software agents, and agentic AI are:

*   **Traditional AI** is tool-centric and functionally narrow, focusing on prediction or classification (Source 3). It has no agency and low context awareness, often being stateless or snapshot-based (Source 1). Its autonomy is limited, often requiring human or external orchestration (Source 2).
*   **Traditional software agents** introduce autonomy and basic communication, but they are often rule-bound or static (Source 3). They operate with implicit or basic agency and moderate context awareness, with some state tracking (Source 1). Their autonomy is medium, operating independently within predefined bounds (Source 2).
*   **Agentic AI** brings together autonomy, asynchrony, and agency, enabling intelligent, goal-driven entities that can reason, act, and adapt within complex systems (Source 3). It operates with explicit agency, purpose, goals, and self-direction, and high context awareness using memory, situational context, and environment models (Source 1). Agentic AI has high autonomy, acting independently with adaptive strategies, and proactivity is a core attribute that drives goal-directed behavior (Source 2).
